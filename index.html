Normalised, a and b are points on the 3-sphere. They appear in the 3D scene as A, B, A⊗B and B⊗A, and the gap between A⊗B and B⊗A is the non-commutativity.

Equivariance under u⊗x

Does f(u⊗x) equal u⊗f(x)? A right-multiplying layer keeps it, and so does a norm-based activation. A componentwise ReLU breaks it. Errors are ‖f(u⊗x) − u⊗f(x)‖ on random inputs. The scene shows, for each network, the first output slot that has a direction (a zero output has none): a large ball at u⊗f(x), a small ball at f(u⊗x), and a bar between them whenever they differ (outputs are normalised onto the 3-sphere). The grey ball is u.

Monge-Ampère on ℂ²

For a Kähler potential K(t) with t = r² = |z₁|² + |z₂|², the determinant is K′(K′ + tK″), and a Ricci-flat metric needs it constant. The legacy engine took a 1×1 Hessian in r instead, which is just φ″(r), and never compared it to anything.

Eguchi-Hanson uses its closed-form K′(t). Whether a single radial potential can describe Taub-NUT at all should be checked against a reference. The fibre factor s = √(1 + tK″/K′) comes from the metric along the Hopf fibres; the scene encodes it by scaling each fibre circle about its centre, which is a visual encoding and not an isometric embedding.
